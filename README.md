# polymarket-updown

Every 15 minutes Polymarket lists a binary market on whether BTC ends the window above where it
started. This repository holds three things built around those markets:

- a dataset of **24,383 resolved markets** (February to October 2026) rebuilt from public APIs,
  with the BTC reference aligned to the second at which each price was observed;
- a study of how well the prices predict the result, and an out-of-sample test of the rules a
  trading bot used in March 2026, including what that bot's orders actually did on chain;
- the event-driven engine that runs the **same strategy objects** in research, paper trading and
  live trading.

## Findings

- **None of the rules has an edge.** Over a seven-month holdout, three of the four underdog
  paths the old bot traded lose 14 to 18% of what they stake, with intervals entirely below zero,
  and the fourth sits at +1% with an interval straddling zero. They were already losing in the
  month they were designed in; the bot's evidence was a handful of trades.
- **The prices are well calibrated.** At every minute the market beats a Brownian model of BTC on
  Brier score, and its calibration slope is within 7% of one. Cheap underdogs are, if anything,
  slightly overpriced: at minute 14, sides priced 10 to 20 cents win 13.5% of the time against an
  average price of 14.7 cents. That bucket is exactly what the old bot's busiest path bought.
- **Polymarket's price history is stamped about 20 seconds late.** Joined to BTC at the stamp, a
  fair-value rule shows a +5.1% holdout edge with p = 0.007. Joined at the time the price was
  actually observed, the same rule loses 7.0%. The lag is measured from the old bot's own live
  samples, and the report reproduces the measurement.
- **The old bot's ledger did not describe its account.** It booked 79 resting limit orders as
  filled. The wallet's on-chain history shows 22 fills, and those lost 34% of their stake; the
  orders that never filled would have made money. That is what adverse selection predicts,
  although 22 fills are too few to prove it.

Everything below is generated by `make report` from the files in `data/`, and CI fails if it
drifts.

### What the original bot actually did

The bot's state file books 79 live orders. They were resting limit orders at the mid, and the wallet's public trade history shows that only 22 of them (28%) ever filled. A resting buy fills when sellers come to it, which tends to be when the price is moving against the side being bought.

| orders | n | win rate | 95% CI | staked | P&L | ROI |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| as booked by the bot (all filled at the limit) | 79 | 0.278 | [0.19, 0.39] | 373.18 | +69.01 | +0.185 |
| actually filled, at the fill price | 22 | 0.182 | [0.07, 0.39] | 103.76 | -35.59 | -0.343 |
| never filled, priced at the limit | 57 | 0.316 | [0.21, 0.44] | 261.88 | +110.71 | +0.423 |

The orders that filled won less often than the ones that did not, as adverse selection predicts, but with 22 fills the gap is not significant on its own (one-sided Fisher exact p = 0.18). What is certain is that the bot's own ledger, which counted every order as filled at its limit, did not describe its account.

## How it works

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    G[Gamma: markets, results] --> E
    C[CLOB: order books] --> E
    B[Binance: BTC reference] --> E
    E[Engine: one snapshot a minute] --> S[Strategy.decide]
    S --> R[RiskGate]
    R --> X{Executor}
    X -- paper --> P[sweep the live book]
    X -- live --> L[fill-and-kill order]
    E --> K[Sinks: ledger, collector, Telegram]
    D[(data/*.parquet)] --> Y[Replay] --> S
    Y --> T[Statistics and report]
```

Design choices worth knowing about:

1. **One strategy object, three drivers.** `Strategy.decide(CycleState)` sees only the snapshots
   observed so far in a cycle. The live engine and the research replay both feed it one snapshot at
   a time and route only the first intent, so there is no separate backtest logic to drift from
   what trades.
2. **Point-in-time data.** A snapshot is the last price stamped at or before the grid time, never
   after. Polymarket stamps its price history about 20 s late, so the BTC reference is read from
   Binance's one-second archive at the second the price was actually observed, and the model
   counts the time left from there.
3. **Costs from the market itself.** Each market carries its own taker-fee curve (the data spans
   two fee schedules and two resolution rules). Spread is an explicit parameter, and every result
   is shown across a range of it.
4. **Inference that respects dependence.** ROI intervals resample whole UTC days, p-values are
   Holm-adjusted across rules, and the holdout starts the day after the old rules were last
   changed.
5. **Risk outside the strategy.** Every intent goes through `RiskGate` (stake cap, price band, one
   position per market, open-position cap, worst-case daily loss, kill-switch file) before any
   executor sees it. Live orders need both `--live` and `UPDOWN_LIVE=1`, and secrets never print.

## Reproduce

```bash
uv sync --all-extras
make check test   # ruff, mypy --strict, pytest
make report       # recompute results/ and the Results section from data/
make data         # optional: rebuild data/ from public APIs (about 10 minutes, 180 MB of cache)
```

## Run

```bash
uv run updown collect                        # record snapshots, top of book and results
uv run updown paper --strategy legacy        # paper trade the old rules against live books
cp .env.example .env                         # then fill it in and export it
UPDOWN_LIVE=1 uv run updown live --live --strategy legacy --max-stake 5
touch runs/KILL                              # the risk gate stops trading on this file
```

Live orders go through Polymarket's official SDK (`polymarket-client`). Check that Polymarket is
available where you live before running anything live.

## Limitations

- Research prices come from Polymarket's one-minute price history, not from order books. The
  history's 20-second stamping lag is measured where independent live samples exist (February
  and March); the stamps' timing shifts in later months, so the lag there is assumed, not
  measured. `updown collect` records real books going forward.
- Research fills are mid plus a half-spread, at any size. Real books are thinner at the extremes.
- The reference is Binance; markets resolve on Chainlink.
- Decisions are made once a minute. Faster participants are not modelled, so any edge measured
  here is an upper bound on what a minute-level strategy could capture.
- The live executor has only run against a fake order client in the tests.
